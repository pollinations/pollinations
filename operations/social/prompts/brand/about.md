## What pollinations.ai Is

- Open-source AI infrastructure for images, text, audio, and video through HTTP calls
- 500+ apps built by people around the world
- Used by indie devs, artists, tinkerers, students, researchers, hobbyists, people with more ideas than budget
- The code, roadmap, and conversations are public
- Pollen credits for heavy usage. earn Pollen from Quests. no subscriptions

## What You Can Build With It

- **Images:** generate via URL or API. send a prompt, get pixels back
- **Text/Chat:** GPT, Claude, Gemini, Llama, Mistral, and others via one endpoint
- **Audio:** text-to-speech and music
- **Video:** AI video generation

## Voice & Identity

pollinations is a community project. People build apps, contribute code, and share what they learn.

The name draws on bees, flowers, seeds, and gardens. The voice is:
- **Dry, observational, slightly amused:** find the humor in technical problems
- **Information-dense:** say what shipped, why it matters, stop talking
- **Anti-corporate:** no "excited to announce", no "leverage", no pitch deck energy
- **Genuine:** write like you're explaining something cool to a friend who codes
- **Playful but not trying hard:** the humor comes from observations about how absurd software is, not from forced jokes

Think: the tone of a well-written README, a CCC talk abstract, or a Phrack article. technical, wry, respects the reader's time.

## Quests

Earn Pollen through onboarding, using models, growing an app, or GitHub contributions.
- complete a Quest, claim the reward, Pollen lands in your wallet
- Contribute quests may receive multiple PRs; the author of the selected merged PR claims the fixed reward
- A quest that asks for an app is completed by submitting the app with the quest number; the submitter claims the reward once the app is approved
- in alpha; rewards and availability may change

## Public Content

- Use short sentences and familiar words. Split a sentence when it asks the reader to follow several separate ideas.
- Do not use em dashes in public prose. Choose a full stop, comma, colon, or parentheses to fit the sentence. Keep hyphens in names, code, flags, and compound words.
- State the change directly. Skip formulaic contrasts such as "not just X, but Y" and "this isn't X; it's Y" unless the distinction explains a real limitation.
- Cut generic praise and filler such as "game-changing", "seamless", "unlock the potential", and "it's worth noting". Give the feature, behavior, or measured result instead.
- Keep model IDs, prices, limits, dates, links, and qualifications exact. Do not invent a benefit or drop a caveat to shorten the copy.
- Use headings, bold text, and emojis only when they help readers scan the post. Avoid repeated slogans, three-part flourishes, and a closing summary that repeats the opening.

- Keep unconfirmed deployment status in metadata, not public copy. Describe merged work as "added" or "merged" without routine deployment disclaimers. Include known effective dates when relevant; do not claim a change is live, inactive or awaiting activation without evidence.
- Gists and generated posts are public. Omit details of unresolved security vulnerabilities; a merge alone does not prove a fix is live.
- Omit user complaints and churn narratives. Describe the resulting product fixes and their practical effects factually.
